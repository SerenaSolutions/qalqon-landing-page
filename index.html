You Haven't Noticed Yet

Brazil changed the composition of regular petrol. The percentage moved from E30 to E32. But the bigger question is what that change means for consumers, prices, vehicles and transparency.

QALQON Editorial Desk · August 2026 · International Edition
The number everyone should know
32%

The mandatory share of anhydrous ethanol in Brazil's regular petrol, effective from 1 August 2026.

01 / What changed?

The fuel at the pump is no longer the same.

Brazil temporarily increased the mandatory percentage of anhydrous ethanol blended into regular petrol from 30% to 32%.

The new E32 blend entered into force on 1 August 2026, for an initial period of 180 days, with the possibility of one extension.

Important distinction

A 32% ethanol blend does NOT mean that petrol should automatically become 32% cheaper.

02 / Follow the money

What should happen to the price?

This is where the public conversation often goes wrong.

The pump price cannot be calculated simply by subtracting the percentage of ethanol from the price of petrol.

The final price reflects the weighted cost of the fuel components, taxes, transportation, storage, distribution, refining, margins and market conditions.

The consumer question

If the composition changes, where is the transparent calculation showing the economic impact?

03 / Global comparison

Brazil vs. the United States

Ethanol-blended petrol is not unique to Brazil. What differs is how each market regulates the blends, their permitted uses and consumer information.

The United States does not use a single ethanol blend for every vehicle and application. E15, for example, has specific regulatory conditions and labelling rules.

04 / The vehicle question

What does E32 mean for engines?

Compatibility depends on the vehicle or equipment, its design and the fuel specification approved by the manufacturer.

Brazil's Ministry of Mines and Energy says technical testing was conducted on representative light vehicles and motorcycles, including evaluations of performance, drivability, cold starts, fuel consumption and emissions.

Flex-fuel vehicles are specifically designed to operate across a broad range of ethanol concentrations. Other equipment should always follow the manufacturer's approved fuel specification.

The question isn't "Is ethanol good or bad?"
The question is: "What are the numbers?"
05 / Myth vs. fact

Separate the headline from the evidence.

MYTH

"32% ethanol means petrol should automatically cost 32% less."

FACT

The final price depends on the relative costs of the components plus taxes, logistics, refining, distribution and margins.

MYTH

"Every petrol engine can safely use any ethanol blend."

FACT

Compatibility depends on the vehicle or equipment and the manufacturer's approved fuel specification.

06 / What QALQON is asking

Four questions the public deserves answered.

01

Show the composition.

Make the fuel composition easy for consumers to understand.

02

Show the economics.

Explain how the composition change affects the cost structure and final price.

03

Document compatibility.

Make technical testing and approved applications accessible to consumers.

04

Make warnings visible.

Consumers should not need to be engineers to understand what they are putting into their vehicles.

07 / The bigger story

This is not just about petrol.

It is about transparency.

Whenever governments, markets or industries change what consumers receive, the public deserves to know:

What changed?
What does it cost?
What are the consequences?

Those are not political questions. They are consumer questions.
